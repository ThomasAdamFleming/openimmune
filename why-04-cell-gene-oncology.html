Cell and gene therapy in oncology

The frontier of cell therapy, where durability is everything, presented with the nuance that separates a serious case from an opportunistic one.

04
Why this matters

Making the next generation last longer

Engineered cell therapies such as CAR-T have achieved what once seemed out of reach: lasting remissions in blood cancers that had exhausted every other option. But the benefit often depends on the modified cells surviving in the body long enough to finish their work. The immune system can recognise part of the therapy, frequently a small non-human component, as foreign and clear it too soon, and when that happens the cancer can come back.

For patients who reach these treatments as a last resort, that durability is everything. Being able to predict which designs the immune system will tolerate, and in whom, would help make the next generation of cell therapies last longer and work for more people, rather than leaving success partly to chance.

What is happening, stated plainly

Durability is the honest angle

Engineered cell therapies can themselves provoke an immune response. Responses against a therapy's non-human components limit how long the cells persist, and the option of re-treatment. Bispecific engagers and antibody-drug conjugates carry their own liabilities.

Much classical oncology immunogenicity is muted by immunosuppression and by historically short courses of treatment, and a large parallel public effort is already working on the related MHC Class I question in cancer. The most defensible focus here is complementary: the durability of cellular therapies, and the Class II and anti-drug-antibody problem alongside it.

The evidence

Persistence is the endpoint that matters

The signal here is real but assay-sensitive, and is best read through durability rather than raw antibody rates.

  • 93% twelve-month relapse-free survival with a humanised CAR construct, against 50 per cent for the earlier murine one: humanised versions were developed precisely to address loss of persistence. Maude et al., J Clin Oncol 2021
  • 91% of patients carried pre-existing antibodies to the murine component of one CAR-T therapy, while a comparable product reads near zero, a reminder that assay design drives much of the apparent variation. Alfar et al., 2025
  • 97 to 100% anti-drug antibodies seen with some solid-tumour bispecific engagers, against under 2 per cent for one that itself depletes B cells: the clearest sign that molecular format drives immunogenicity. Zhou et al., J Immunother Cancer 2022
Where it applies

A large and fast-growing group

Recipients of engineered cell therapies across blood cancers and, increasingly, solid tumours.

  • CAR-T recipients (blood cancers)
  • Solid-tumour cell therapies (emerging)
  • Bispecific-engager and ADC recipients

The global CAR-T market was around $6.5 billion in 2024 and is growing at roughly 30 per cent a year, with more than 20,000 cumulative treatments recorded in Europe alone.

The strongest immunogenicity story here is durability: designs the immune system tolerates, in the people who will tolerate them, so the next generation of cell therapies lasts longer and reaches more patients.
