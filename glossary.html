Glossary

Plain definitions of the terms used across this site, for readers coming to immunogenicity for the first time and for those who want the programme's language pinned down.

The science

Terms used about the immune response

Biologic medicine
A medicine made of large molecules, usually proteins, produced in living cells rather than synthesised chemically. Antibody therapies, replacement enzymes, clotting factors, and cell and gene therapies are all biologics.
Immunogenicity
The tendency of a medicine to provoke the patient's own immune system against it. Explained in full on What is immunogenicity?
Anti-drug antibody (ADA)
An antibody a patient's immune system produces against a medicine they have been given. Explained in full on What is an anti-drug antibody?
Binding antibody
An anti-drug antibody that attaches to the medicine without blocking its action. It usually causes the drug to be cleared faster rather than stopping it working.
Neutralising antibody
An anti-drug antibody that attaches where the medicine does its work and blocks it. The drug can still be present and have no effect.
Inhibitor
The term used in haemophilia care for a neutralising antibody against replacement clotting factor. It is the most feared complication of treatment.
Immune tolerance
The immune system's ability to recognise something as acceptable and leave it alone. A medicine that is tolerated keeps working; immunogenicity is what happens when tolerance is not established or is lost.
Immune-recognition genetics
The inherited variation that governs which protein fragments a person's immune system presents and reacts to. It differs substantially between individuals and between populations, which is why the same medicine can be tolerated by one person and rejected by another.
Sequence
The order of amino acids that defines a protein. Predicting immune response from sequence, rather than discovering it in patients, is the capability OpenImmune is working towards.
Multimodal dataset
A dataset that brings different layers of measurement together for the same subjects, so that patterns invisible in any single layer become learnable.
The programme

Terms used about how this work is built

Focused Research Organisation (FRO)
A not-for-profit set up to deliver one defined scientific goal, openly, and then conclude. It exists to do work that is too large and too coordinated for a single laboratory, and too far from a product for any one company to fund and give away. OpenImmune is structured this way; see About.
Open by design
Releasing every output as a public good from the outset, rather than publishing selectively after the fact. It is a decision about structure, not a policy applied at the end.
FAIR principles
A standard for research data holding that it should be findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable. OpenImmune releases its outputs under these principles so that others can build on them directly.
Public good
Something whose value is diffuse enough that no single organisation will pay to build it and then give it away, even where all of them need it. That is the gap this programme exists to close.
Neutral funding
Funding from government and philanthropy rather than from any one company, which is what keeps the result open, general, and in the public interest. Neutral money builds the commons; commercial money builds on it.
Build and validation
The description of this first phase. It is a standalone scientific resource, valuable and publishable in its own right, rather than a preliminary run at something larger.
