Why this matters · 01 · Immune-mediated inflammatory disease

Immune-mediated inflammatory disease

The largest and most direct application: the biologics that give millions an ordinary life, and quietly stop working for many.

01
Why this matters

A confident first choice, in place of guesswork

For millions of people with rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease, colitis or psoriasis, a biologic medicine is what makes an ordinary life possible: work, family, a night's sleep without pain. The difficulty is that, for a substantial number, the drug quietly stops working. Their immune system learns to treat the medicine as foreign and neutralises it, the disease returns, and they begin the slow, dispiriting business of switching from one costly drug to the next, with no way of knowing in advance which will hold.

Each failed switch can mean months of avoidable flare-ups. Being able to predict, before the first dose, which biologic a person's immune system will accept would replace that guesswork with a confident first choice, sparing patients relapse and the health system a great deal of waste.

What is happening

Ideal conditions for an anti-drug response

These medicines are given repeatedly, over years, to people who are not otherwise immunosuppressed, which is close to ideal conditions for an anti-drug response. Anti-drug antibodies are the dominant cause of secondary loss of response, the familiar clinical pattern in which a biologic works and then stops.

The fact that co-prescribed drugs such as methotrexate partly suppress this is itself evidence that the mechanism is immunological, and part of the risk is host-encoded, written into a person's own genetics.

The evidence

Common, costly, and partly predictable

  • 62.8% of patients on infliximab monotherapy developed anti-drug antibodies by three years in a large prospective study, one of the clearest measures of how common this is. Kennedy et al., Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2019
  • 47 to 65% the reduction in anti-drug antibody formation when an immunomodulator is co-prescribed, direct evidence that the mechanism is immunological. Garces et al., Ann Rheum Dis 2013
  • 1.9× the increase in anti-drug antibody risk carried by one common inherited variant, present in around 40 per cent of Europeans. This is exactly the sequence-plus-genetics signal a model can learn. Sazonovs et al., Gastroenterology 2020
  • $41 to 44bn the annual market for TNF inhibitors alone, within a broader anti-inflammatory biologics market of around $105 billion, much of it spent cycling through drugs as each in turn fails. Precedence Research
Where it applies

Tens of millions of people worldwide

Chronic, repeat-dosing conditions treated with biologics across large, well-studied populations.

  • Rheumatoid arthritis
  • Psoriatic arthritis
  • Juvenile arthritis
  • Ankylosing spondylitis
  • Crohn's disease
  • Ulcerative colitis
  • Plaque psoriasis
  • Non-infectious uveitis

Roughly 17.6 million people live with rheumatoid arthritis, more than 10 million with inflammatory bowel disease, and well over 100 million with psoriasis. Loss of response runs at around 13 to 21 per cent per patient-year (Jharap et al., Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2022), so the number of people affected at any moment is large.

This is where a prediction from sequence and a person's own genetics would be felt most directly: a confident first-choice biologic, in place of expensive, sequential cycling through drugs as each one fails.
