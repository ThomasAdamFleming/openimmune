About OpenImmune

Making the immune response to medicines predictable

A UK-led, not-for-profit research programme building an open scientific foundation, and sharing it.

What it is

What OpenImmune is

OpenImmune is a UK-led, not-for-profit research programme building an open scientific foundation for predicting the immunogenicity of biologic medicines: whether the body's immune system will attack a treatment.

Some of our most effective medicines can fail this way, and today that failure is hard to predict. OpenImmune is building the multimodal dataset and the generalisable model that make it predictable, and releasing them openly for everyone to build on.

How it is built

A Focused Research Organisation

OpenImmune is structured as a Focused Research Organisation: a not-for-profit set up to deliver one defined scientific goal, openly, and then conclude.

It is built to do what neither academia nor industry will do alone. The work is too large and too coordinated for a single laboratory, and too far from a product for any one company to fund and then give away. A not-for-profit structure is what keeps the work neutral and in the public interest: it holds the results on behalf of everyone, contracts specialist partners to do the work, and releases its outputs openly as a public good.

Leadership

The people behind it

A small team with deep experience across autonomous laboratories, immunology, artificial intelligence, pharmaceutical development, and clinical data.

Tom Fleming
Founder and CEO

Tom founded OpenImmune and leads it as CEO. A chemist with an executive MBA, he spent a decade co-founding and leading Arctoris, the Oxford autonomous drug discovery company, and advises UK government on science, AI, and sovereign capability. He is a Fellow of the Royal Societies of Chemistry, Biology, and Arts.

Dr Timothy Hickling
Immunogenicity science

A leading authority on the clinical immunogenicity of therapeutic proteins, with more than two decades in biologics safety, Tim advises on the scientific direction of the programme.
